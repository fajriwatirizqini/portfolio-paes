# Dea Bakery Mobile App Case Study

## Project Overview

### Role
UI/UX Designer

### Duration
4 Weeks

### Platform
Mobile Application (iOS & Android)

### Project Type
Food & Beverage Ordering Experience

### Objective

Design a mobile ordering experience that helps customers discover bakery products, place orders efficiently, and enjoy a seamless purchasing journey from product exploration to checkout.

---

# The Business Context

Dea Bakery has built a strong presence through its physical stores, where customers can directly browse products and make purchases.

As customer expectations shifted toward digital convenience, the business needed a mobile experience that could replicate the attractiveness of in-store browsing while enabling customers to order products anytime and anywhere.

The challenge was not only to digitize the purchasing process but also to preserve the emotional experience of discovering freshly baked products while reducing friction throughout the customer journey.

---

# The Problem

Through analysis of common food-ordering behaviors and bakery purchasing patterns, several challenges were identified.

## Product Discovery Friction

Customers often browse bakery products without a specific item in mind. A large product catalog can quickly become overwhelming if products are not organized and presented effectively.

## Ordering Complexity

Long or unclear ordering processes create unnecessary friction and increase the likelihood of users abandoning their purchase.

## Limited Product Confidence

Unlike physical stores, customers cannot directly inspect products before purchasing. The application must compensate for this limitation through strong visual presentation and clear information.

## Mobile-First Expectations

Modern users expect fast, intuitive, and familiar mobile experiences similar to leading e-commerce and food-ordering applications.

---

# Research & Discovery

To better understand user expectations, the project included competitive benchmarking and analysis of digital purchasing behavior across food and beverage applications.

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Visuals Drive Purchase Decisions

Users rely heavily on product images when evaluating bakery products.

### Finding 2: Exploration is Part of the Experience

Many customers browse categories before deciding what to purchase.

### Finding 3: Simplicity Encourages Conversion

The fewer steps required to complete a purchase, the more likely users are to complete their order.

### Finding 4: Multi-Item Purchases Are Common

Customers frequently purchase several products during a single transaction.

---

# Design Challenge

## How might we create a mobile bakery experience that encourages product discovery while allowing customers to complete purchases quickly and confidently?

---

# Design Principles

The solution was guided by three core design principles.

## 1. Visual-First Product Discovery

Product imagery should become the primary focus of the browsing experience, similar to how customers are attracted by bakery displays in physical stores.

## 2. Simplified Purchase Journey

The ordering process should minimize effort and support fast decision-making.

## 3. Confidence Through Clarity

Customers should always understand what they are purchasing, how much it costs, and how to complete their order.

---

# Design Exploration

## Product Browsing Experience

### Challenge

Users needed an easy way to explore products without feeling overwhelmed by the catalog.

### Solution

A category-based browsing structure was introduced to help users quickly navigate products based on their interests and purchase intent.

### Design Rationale

- Reduces search effort
- Encourages product exploration
- Improves discoverability of bakery items

---

## Product Detail Experience

### Challenge

Customers cannot physically inspect products before purchase.

### Solution

Product detail screens prioritize high-quality imagery, clear descriptions, pricing, and supporting information.

### Design Rationale

- Builds purchase confidence
- Reduces uncertainty
- Supports informed decision-making

---

## Cart & Checkout Experience

### Challenge

Complex checkout processes often lead to abandonment.

### Solution

A streamlined checkout flow was designed with clear progression, prominent call-to-action buttons, and minimal distractions.

### Design Rationale

- Reduces cognitive load
- Increases checkout efficiency
- Supports faster order completion

---

# Final Solution

The final design focuses on creating a balance between visual appeal and usability.

### Intuitive Product Discovery

Customers can browse products through a visually engaging and well-organized catalog.

### Faster Ordering Journey

The ordering process is streamlined from product selection to checkout.

### Stronger Purchase Confidence

Clear product information and visual presentation support purchasing decisions.

### Mobile-Friendly Experience

The interface is optimized for touch interactions and mobile usability patterns.

# Reflection

This project reinforced the importance of designing for both emotional engagement and functional efficiency.

Bakery purchases are often influenced by visual appeal and spontaneous decision-making. However, customers still expect a frictionless digital experience when completing transactions.

The biggest design challenge was balancing product attractiveness with usability, ensuring that users could enjoy browsing while still completing purchases quickly and confidently.
